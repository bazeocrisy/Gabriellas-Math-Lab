Adding with Place Value

Goal: I can add tenths to tenths and hundredths to hundredths.
Show how

Add 0.23 + 0.45.

0.23
+
0.45

0.68
  1. Tenths with tenths: 2 tenths + 4 tenths = 6 tenths (the full columns).
  2. Hundredths with hundredths: 3 hundredths + 5 hundredths = 8 hundredths (the extra squares).
  3. Put them together: 6 tenths and 8 hundredths = 0.68
Why it works: you can only add things of the same size, like adding apples to apples. Tenths add to tenths; hundredths add to hundredths.

4.2

Line Up and Add

Goal: I can line up the decimal points and add in columns.
Show how

Add 3.42 + 1.35.

  1. Line up the decimal points, one under the other. Then every place is in its own column.
  2. Start at the right. Hundredths: 2 + 5 = 7.
  3. Tenths: 4 + 3 = 7. Ones: 3 + 1 = 4.
  4. Bring the decimal point straight down: 4.77
Why it works: lining up the decimal points lines up the places, so you add hundredths to hundredths and tenths to tenths, just like in 4.1.

4.3

Adding with Regrouping

Goal: I can regroup 10 of one place as 1 of the next place.
Show how

Solve 3.45 + 2.27.

  1. Line up the decimal points.
  2. Add the hundredths: 5 + 7 = 12 hundredths. That is 1 tenth and 2 hundredths: write 2, carry the 1 to the tenths.
  3. Add the tenths, including the carried 1: 4 + 2 + 1 = 7.
  4. Add the ones: 3 + 2 = 5.
  5. Place the decimal point straight down: 5.72
Why it works: 10 hundredths make 1 tenth (Section 1). So 12 hundredths is 1 tenth + 2 hundredths. Any place works the same way: 9.6 + 2.7 → tenths 6 + 7 = 13: write 3, carry 1 to the ones → 12.3. Sometimes you carry twice.

Different Numbers of Decimal Places

Goal: I can add decimals like 4.6 + 2.35 by writing placeholder zeros.
Show how

Add 4.6 + 2.35.

  1. Line up the decimal points, not the last digits. The 6 goes over the 3 (both tenths).
  2. 4.6 has no hundredths digit. Write a placeholder zero: 4.6 = 4.60 (Section 1: equivalent decimals).
  3. Add: hundredths 0 + 5 = 5, tenths 6 + 3 = 9, ones 4 + 2 = 6 → 6.95
Watch out: lining up the right edges (4.6 over 2.35 with the 6 over the 5) adds tenths to hundredths, which gives a wrong answer. A whole number has its point at the end: 5 = 5.00.

4.5

Estimate to Check

Goal: I can round to estimate a sum and check that my answer makes sense.
Show how

Is 5.72 a reasonable answer for 3.45 + 2.27?

  1. Round each number to the nearest whole number (Section 3): 3.45 ≈ 3 and 2.27 ≈ 2.
  2. Add the rounded numbers: 3 + 2 = 5. The exact sum should be close to 5.
  3. Compare: 5.72 is close to 5, so it is reasonable. If you had written 57.2 or 0.572, the estimate shows the decimal point is in the wrong place.
Why it works: rounded numbers are easy to add in your head, and they are close to the real numbers, so their sum is close to the real sum. An estimate catches decimal-point mistakes.

Addition Word Problems

Goal: I can tell when a story needs adding, then add and label the answer.
Show how

Leo ran 2.6 km on Monday and 3.75 km on Tuesday. How far did he run in all?

  1. What is asked? The total distance. “In all” and “total” mean add.
  2. Estimate: 3 + 4 = about 7 km.
  3. Line up and add: 2.60 + 3.75 = 6.35.
  4. Check and label: 6.35 is close to 7. Leo ran 6.35 km.
Adding words: in all, total, altogether, combined, both, sum, how much … together.
