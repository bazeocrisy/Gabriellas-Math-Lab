5.1

Line Up and Subtract

Goal: I can subtract decimals place by place.
Show how

Subtract 6.85 − 2.43.

  1. Line up the decimal points. Bigger number on top.
  2. Start at the right. Hundredths: 5 − 3 = 2.
  3. Tenths: 8 − 4 = 4. Ones: 6 − 2 = 4.
  4. Bring the decimal point straight down: 4.42
Why it works: same as adding: lining up the points means you take hundredths from hundredths and tenths from tenths.

5.2

Subtracting with Regrouping

Goal: I can regroup 1 of a place as 10 of the next smaller place.
Show how

Solve 8.20 − 3.76.

Small red numbers show each regrouped digit.

  1. Line up the points. Start with the hundredths: 0 − 6? Not enough.
  2. Regroup a tenth: 2 tenths become 1 tenth; the hundredths get 10. Now 10 − 6 = 4.
  3. Tenths: 1 − 7? Not enough. Regroup a one: 8 ones become 7; the tenths get 10 more: 11 − 7 = 4.
  4. Ones: 7 − 3 = 4. Bring the point down: 4.44
Why it works: 1 one = 10 tenths and 1 tenth = 10 hundredths. Regrouping trades one bigger piece for ten smaller pieces, so the top number keeps the same value: 8.20 = 7 ones + 11 tenths + 10 hundredths.

5.3

Placeholder Zeros and Different Lengths

Goal: I can subtract when the numbers have different numbers of decimal places.
Show how

Subtract 5 − 2.36.

  1. A whole number has its point at the end: 5 = 5.00. Write the zeros so both numbers have hundredths.
  2. Hundredths: 0 − 6? The tenths are 0 too, so regroup a one: 5 ones → 4 ones and 10 tenths.
  3. Now regroup a tenth: 10 tenths → 9 tenths and 10 hundredths.
  4. Subtract: 10 − 6 = 4, 9 − 3 = 6, 4 − 2 = 2 → 2.64
Why it works: 5.00 has the same value as 5 (Section 1). The zeros give you places to regroup into. Never take the bottom number from the top just because it is “bigger-looking”.

5.4

Check Subtraction by Adding

Goal: I can use addition to check a subtraction answer.
Show how

Check: is 8.20 − 3.76 = 4.44 correct?

  1. Subtraction takes a part away from the whole: 8.20 − 3.76 = 4.44.
  2. So the two parts must add back to the whole: 4.44 + 3.76 should equal 8.20.
  3. Add: 4.44 + 3.76 = 8.20. It matches, so the subtraction is correct.
Why it works: answer + number you subtracted = number you started with. If the sum does not match, find the column where it goes wrong.

5.5

Subtraction Word Problems

Goal: I can tell when a story needs subtracting, then subtract and label.
Show how

Mia had $10.00. She spent $6.45. How much money does she have left?

  1. What is asked? How much is left. “Left”, “how much more”, and “difference” mean subtract.
  2. Estimate: 10 − 6 = about $4.
  3. Line up and subtract: 10.00 − 6.45 = 3.55 (regroup across the zeros).
  4. Check and label: 3.55 + 6.45 = 10.00 ✓. Mia has $3.55 left.
Subtracting words: left, remain, how much more, how much less, how much taller/longer/heavier, difference, change.
