The Decimal Point

Goal: I can tell the whole-number part from the part less than one.
Show how

Look at 4.735. What is the whole-number part? Between which two whole numbers is it?

4.735
whole-number
part decimal
point part less
than one
  1. Find the decimal point.
  2. Digits to the left are the whole-number part: 4. That is 4 wholes.
  3. Digits to the right are the part less than one: 0.735.
  4. 4 wholes plus a part less than one: 4.735 is between 4 and 5.
Why it works: the decimal point separates whole things (left) from parts of a whole (right). Read it as “and”: 4.735 is 4 and 735 thousandths.

Show how
10 equal parts: each is 1 tenth = 0.1
100 squares: each is 1 hundredth = 0.01
1 square in 10 slices: 1 thousandth = 0.001

What decimal does this grid show?

  1. The whole grid is 1 whole cut into 100 equal squares.
  2. Count the full columns. Each column is 1 tenth: 3 tenths.
  3. Count the extra squares. Each is 1 hundredth: 4 hundredths.
  4. Write the digits in their places: 0.34 34 squares ✓
Why it works: 10 tenths, 100 hundredths, or 1,000 thousandths each make 1 whole. Shading fills one column at a time, top to bottom.

Naming the Place of a Digit

Goal: I can name the place of any digit in a decimal.
Show how

In 4.735, what is the place of the 3?

  1. Write the number in a place-value chart. The decimal point lines up with the chart's point.
  2. Count places to the right of the point: 1st is tenths, 2nd is hundredths, 3rd is thousandths. To the left: 1st is ones, 2nd is tens.
  3. The 3 is the 2nd digit after the point. It is in the hundredths place.
  4. Other way round: which digit is in the thousandths place? Count 3 places right: the 5.
Why it works: every place has a name. Places right of the point end in “-ths” because they are parts of one.

Finding the Value of a Digit

Goal: I can find how much a digit is worth.
Show how

What is the value of the 7 in 4.735?

  1. Locate the digit: find the 7 in 4.735.
  2. Identify its place: the 7 is the 1st digit after the point, the tenths place.
  3. Find its value: 7 tenths = 7 × 0.1 = 0.7
Why it works: a digit's value depends on its place. The same 7 would be worth 0.07 in the hundredths place, or 7 in the ones place.

Reading a Place-Value Chart

Goal: I can write the number a chart shows, and put a number into a chart.
Show how

What number does this chart show?

  1. Read from the left: 3 ones. Write 3.
  2. The decimal point comes right after the ones: 3.
  3. The tenths place is empty. An empty place before the last digit gets a 0: 3.0
  4. Then 5 hundredths: 3.05 not 3.5!
Why it works: the 0 holds the tenths place so the 5 stays in the hundredths place. Without it, 3.5 would mean 5 tenths.

Each Place Is 10 Times the Next

Goal: I can use “10 of one place make 1 of the place to its left.”
Show how

How many hundredths are in 3 tenths?

  1. 1 tenth is one column. One column has 10 small squares, so 1 tenth = 10 hundredths.
  2. 3 tenths = 3 × 10 hundredths = 30 hundredths. 0.3 = 0.30 ✓
  3. Two places: 1 tenth = 10 hundredths = 100 thousandths (10 × 10).
  4. Other way: group by 10s. 40 hundredths = 4 groups of 10 hundredths = 4 tenths.
How many times as much? The 4 in 0.4 is 1 place left of the 4 in 0.04, so it is worth 10 times as much. 2 places left: 10 × 10 = 100 times. Going right, 1 place is worth one tenth as much: 0.04 is 1/10 of 0.4.

Expanded Form

Goal: I can write a decimal as the sum of its place values.
Show how

Write 4.735 in expanded form.

  1. Find the value of each digit (Skill 1.4): 4, 0.7, 0.03, 0.005.
  2. Write the values with + between them, largest place first.
  3. A 0 digit adds nothing, so skip it: 6.208 = 6 + 0.2 + 0.008.

4 + 0.7 + 0.03 + 0.005

Why it works: a decimal is the total of its digits' values. Expanded form shows each part. Standard form (4.735) is the short way to write the total.

From Expanded Form to Standard Form

Goal: I can write the standard form of a number given in expanded form.
Show how

Write 5 + 0.2 + 0.009 in standard form.

  1. Put each value's digit in its place: 5 ones, 2 tenths, 9 thousandths.
  2. No hundredths are listed, so write a 0 in the hundredths place (highlighted).
  3. Read the chart: 5.209 not 5.29!
Why it works: expanded form lists the value in each place. Standard form writes each digit in its place, with 0 in any empty place before the last digit.

Equivalent Decimals

Goal: I can tell when two decimals name the same amount.
Show how

Is 0.5 equal to 0.50?

0.5 = 5 tenths
=
0.50 = 50 hundredths
  1. 0.5 is 5 tenths. 1 tenth = 10 hundredths, so 5 tenths = 50 hundredths = 0.50.
  2. The same amount is shaded. Yes: 0.5 = 0.50 = 0.500.
  3. Watch out: a zero right after the point is different. 0.05 is 5 hundredths, not equal to 0.5.
Why it works: a zero added at the end, after the last decimal digit, does not move any digit, so the value stays the same.
