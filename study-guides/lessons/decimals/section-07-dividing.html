7.1

Dividing by 10, 100, and 1,000

Goal: I can divide by 10, 100, or 1,000 by moving digits to smaller places.
Show how

Divide 34.5 ÷ 100.

→
  1. Dividing by 100 makes each digit worth one hundredth as much: it moves 2 places to the right.
  2. 3 tens → 3 tenths, 4 ones → 4 hundredths, 5 tenths → 5 thousandths.
  3. 34.5 ÷ 100 = 0.345. Shortcut: count the zeros (100 has 2), move the decimal point that many places to the left. Write a 0 in the ones place.
Why it works: dividing undoes multiplying. × 100 moves digits 2 places left (Section 6), so ÷ 100 moves them 2 places right. Fill empty places with zeros: 7 ÷ 100 = 0.07.

Decimal ÷ Whole Number: Equal Groups

Goal: I can share a decimal into equal groups using place value.
Show how

Solve 6.4 ÷ 2.

6.4
Group 1
Group 2
Written
  1. 6.4 ÷ 2 means share 6.4 into 2 equal groups.
  2. Think in tenths: 6.4 is 64 tenths.
  3. 64 tenths ÷ 2 = 32 tenths in each group.
  4. 32 tenths = 3.2. Each group gets 3 ones and 2 tenths.
  5. Written way (left): put the point up first. 6 ÷ 2 = 3, then 4 ÷ 2 = 2 → 3.2. Same answer.
Why it works: sharing 6 ones and 4 tenths into 2 groups gives 3 ones and 2 tenths in each group. Thinking “64 tenths” turns the problem into whole-number division.

Decimal ÷ Whole Number: Long Division

Goal: I can use long division and put the point straight up.
Show how

Solve 7.56 ÷ 3.

  1. Put the decimal point in the answer first, straight above the point in 7.56.
  2. Divide like whole numbers: 7 ÷ 3 = 2, remainder 1.
  3. Bring down the 5: 15 ÷ 3 = 5. Bring down the 6: 6 ÷ 3 = 2.
  4. Answer: 2.52. Check: 2.52 × 3 = 7.56 ✓
Why it works: the remainder 1 one becomes 10 tenths, so 10 + 5 = 15 tenths. Long division keeps trading leftovers into the next smaller place, just like regrouping.

7.4

Whole Numbers with Decimal Answers

Goal: I can keep dividing past the point by writing zeros.
Show how

Solve 7 ÷ 4.

  1. 7 ÷ 4 = 1 with 3 left over. Instead of a remainder, keep going.
  2. Write 7 as 7.00 (equivalent decimals) and put the point up.
  3. 3 ones = 30 tenths: 30 ÷ 4 = 7, 2 left. 2 tenths = 20 hundredths: 20 ÷ 4 = 5.
  4. Answer: 1.75. Check: 1.75 × 4 = 7 ✓
Why it works: adding zeros after the point does not change 7, but it gives the leftover a place to go. Stop when the remainder is 0.

Dividing by a Decimal

Goal: I can change a problem so I divide by a whole number.
Show how

Solve 4.8 ÷ 0.6.

  1. Dividing by a decimal is tricky, so make the divisor a whole number. 0.6 × 10 = 6.
  2. Multiply the other number by the same 10: 4.8 × 10 = 48. Now the problem is 48 ÷ 6.
  3. 48 ÷ 6 = 8. So 4.8 ÷ 0.6 = 8.
  4. Check: 8 × 0.6 = 4.8 ✓. Think: how many 0.6s fit in 4.8? Eight of them.
Why it works: multiplying both numbers by the same amount does not change the answer: 4.8 ÷ 0.6 = 48 ÷ 6 = 480 ÷ 60. These are equivalent division expressions. If the divisor has 2 decimal places, multiply both by 100.

7.6

Check by Multiplying

Goal: I can use multiplication to check a division answer.
Show how

Check: is 7.56 ÷ 3 = 2.52 correct?

  1. Division splits a number into equal groups: 7.56 ÷ 3 = 2.52 means 3 groups of 2.52 make 7.56.
  2. So multiply: 2.52 × 3. As whole numbers: 252 × 3 = 756. Two decimal places: 7.56.
  3. 7.56 matches the number we started with, so the division is correct.
Why it works: quotient × divisor = dividend. If the product does not match, check where the decimal point went in the quotient.

7.7

Division Word Problems

Goal: I can tell when a story needs dividing, then solve and label.
Show how

Three friends share $7.50 equally. How much does each friend get?

  1. What is asked? One share of $7.50 split into 3 equal parts: divide. (“Share equally”, “each”, “per”, “split”, “how many … fit”.)
  2. Estimate: 7.5 ÷ 3 is a little more than 6 ÷ 3 = 2.
  3. Divide: 7.50 ÷ 3 = 2.50.
  4. Check and label: 2.50 × 3 = 7.50 ✓. Each friend gets $2.50.
Two kinds of division stories: sharing (“how much in each group?”) and measuring (“how many groups of 0.5 fit?”). Both use ÷.
