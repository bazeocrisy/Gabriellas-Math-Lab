Words to Know

whole
One complete thing or amount, like a whole bar or a whole grid. One whole is the number 1.
2.35
decimal
A number that uses a decimal point and place value to show parts of a whole, like 0.7 or 2.35.
4.6
decimal point
The dot that separates the whole number (on the left) from the parts of a whole (on the right). Read it as “and.”
tenth
One of 10 equal parts of a whole. One tenth is written 0.1 (or 1/10).
hundredth
One of 100 equal parts of a whole. One hundredth is written 0.01 (or 1/100).
0.001
thousandth
One of 1,000 equal parts of a whole. Cut one hundredth into 10 equal slices: each slice is 0.001.
0.627
place value
The value a digit has because of its place. In 0.627, the 2 is in the hundredths place, so it is worth 0.02.
0.5=0.50
equivalent decimals
Decimals that name the same amount. 0.5 and 0.50 are equivalent: 5 tenths is the same as 50 hundredths.
Say it with place names: say 0.7 as “seven tenths” instead of “point seven.” Saying the place name tells you what the number means.

LearnThe big idea

What Is a Decimal?Decimals use place value to show wholes and parts of a whole: tenths, hundredths, and thousandths.

Start with a chocolate bar

This chocolate bar is one whole. It is made of 10 equal pieces, so each piece is one tenth of the bar.

Gabriella eats 3 pieces. She ate 3 tenths of the bar. As a decimal, that is 0.3. As a fraction, it is 3/10.

The pieces must be equal. If some pieces were bigger, they would not be tenths.

3 of the 10 equal pieces are eaten (dashed). 7 tenths are left.

Why this matters. Look at the hundredths grid: one column has 10 small squares. So 10 hundredths fill exactly 1 tenth. That is why 0.1 = 0.10. Going the other way, each place is one tenth (1/10) of the place to its left.
Where you see decimals: money ($2.35), running distances (0.8 mile), science measurements (0.125 liter), and sports times (9.58 seconds).

Zeros: When They MatterSome zeros change a decimal. Some do not. Pictures show which is which.

Equivalent decimals: 0.5 = 0.50
0.5 = 5 tenths
=
0.50 = 50 hundredths
The same amount is shaded. 5 tenths = 50 hundredths. A zero added at the end, after the decimal digits, does not change the value: 0.5 = 0.50 = 0.500.
A placeholder zero: 0.05 is NOT 0.5
0.05 = 5 hundredths
≠
0.5 = 5 tenths
In 0.05, the zero is a placeholder zero in the tenths place. It holds the place so the 5 stays in the hundredths place. Leave that zero out and the 5 slides into the tenths place: a number 10 times as big.
Watch out: is 0.35 bigger than 0.4?

Some students think 0.35 > 0.4 because 35 is bigger than 4. Look at the grids:

0.4 > 0.35. The tenths agree: 4 tenths > 3 tenths. More digits does not mean a bigger decimal.

Step by Step

Example 1 · Write the decimal for a model

What decimal names the shaded part of the grid?

In every grid in this guide, shading fills one column at a time, top to bottom.

  1. The grid is one whole cut into 100 equal squares, so each square is one hundredth.
  2. Count the full columns. Each column is 1 tenth. There are 3 full columns: 3 tenths.
  3. Count the extra squares. There are 4: 4 hundredths.
  4. Write the digits in their places: 0.34
  5. Check: 34 of the 100 squares are shaded. Read it as “thirty-four hundredths.” ✓ 34 squares!
Example 2 · Show thousandths on a grid

Show 0.125 on a hundredths grid.

  1. Find each digit's value: 1 tenth, 2 hundredths, 5 thousandths.
  2. Shade 1 full column for 1 tenth.
  3. Shade 2 more squares for 2 hundredths. That is 12 squares in all.
  4. Thousandths are smaller than one square. Cut the next square into 10 equal slices and shade 5 slices.
  5. Check: 0.125 is a little more than 12 hundredths and less than 13 hundredths. 12 squares + half a square ✓

Worked ExamplesFollow each step
Example 3 · Find the value of a digit

What is the value of the 8 in 2.385?

  1. Write the number in a place-value chart. Line the decimal point up with the chart's point.
  2. The 8 is the second digit after the decimal point: the hundredths place.
  3. So the 8 is worth 8 hundredths, which is 0.08.

The other digits: 2 ones (2), 3 tenths (0.3), 5 thousandths (0.005). So 2.385 = 2 + 0.3 + 0.08 + 0.005.

Example 4 · Write a decimal from words

Write “seven hundredths” as a decimal.

  1. The last word is hundredths, so the last digit goes in the hundredths place: 2 places after the point.
  2. Put the 7 in the hundredths place. The tenths place is empty, so it gets a placeholder zero.
  3. Answer: 0.07  not 0.7!
Example 5 · Compare two decimals

Which is greater, 0.6 or 0.58?

  1. Line up the decimal points. Give both numbers the same number of places: 0.6 = 0.60.
  2. Compare from the left. Ones: 0 and 0, the same. Tenths: 6 > 5.
  3. The tenths decide it, so 0.6 > 0.58. The grids agree: 60 squares > 58 squares.

Common mistakes to watch for
  • Thinking more digits means bigger (0.35 > 0.4).
  • Dropping a placeholder zero (0.07 written as 0.7).
  • Counting the unshaded part of a model.
  • Mixing up hundredths and thousandths places.
  • Reading “point four three” without the place name; ask for “forty-three hundredths.”
Talk about it (2 minutes)
  • “Show me 0.3 and 0.03 on a grid. Which is bigger? How do you know?”
  • “Why is 0.5 the same as 0.50, but 0.05 is different?”
  • “What is the value of the 4 in 1.246?” (0.04)
