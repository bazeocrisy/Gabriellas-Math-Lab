Multiplying by 10, 100, and 1,000

Goal: I can multiply by 10, 100, or 1,000 by moving digits to bigger places.
Show how

Multiply 3.45 × 100.

→
Hundreds100Tens10Ones1Tenths0.1
345.
  1. 100 = 10 × 10, so each digit becomes worth 10 times, then 10 times again: it moves 2 places to the left.
  2. 3 ones → 3 hundreds, 4 tenths → 4 tens, 5 hundredths → 5 ones.
  3. 3.45 × 100 = 345. Shortcut: count the zeros (100 has 2), move the decimal point that many places to the right.
Why it works: each place is 10 times the place to its right (Section 1). × 10 moves digits 1 place, × 100 moves 2, × 1,000 moves 3. Fill empty places with zeros: 0.7 × 100 = 70.

Decimal × Whole Number: Equal Groups

Goal: I can use equal groups and place value to multiply a decimal by a whole number.
Show how

Solve 2.4 × 3.

Group 1
Group 2
Group 3
  1. 2.4 × 3 means 3 groups of 2.4. Each group is 2 ones and 4 tenths.
  2. Count the ones: 3 × 2 = 6 ones.
  3. Count the tenths: 3 × 4 = 12 tenths. 10 tenths make 1 one, so 12 tenths = 1.2.
  4. Put them together: 6 + 1.2 = 7.2
Why it works: multiplying is adding equal groups: 2.4 + 2.4 + 2.4 = 7.2. Multiply each place, then regroup, just like in addition.

Decimal × Whole Number: Written Method

Goal: I can multiply as whole numbers, then place the decimal point.
Show how

Solve 2.4 × 3 with the written method.

  1. Ignore the decimal point for now. Multiply as whole numbers: 24 × 3 = 72.
  2. Count decimal places in the factors: 2.4 has 1, 3 has 0. Total: 1.
  3. Put 1 decimal place in the answer: 72 → 7.2
  4. Check with 6.2: 3 groups of 2.4 = 7.2 ✓. And 2.4 × 3 is about 2 × 3 = 6, so 7.2 makes sense.
Why it works: 2.4 is 24 tenths. 24 tenths × 3 = 72 tenths = 7.2. The answer is counted in tenths, so it has 1 decimal place.

6.4

Decimal × Decimal: Area Model

Goal: I can use a grid to see why tenths × tenths make hundredths.
Show how

Solve 0.3 × 0.4.

  1. The grid is 1 whole: 100 small squares.
  2. Shade 0.3 of the width (3 columns, blue) and 0.4 of the height (4 rows, pink).
  3. 0.3 × 0.4 is the part where they overlap (dark): 3 × 4 = 12 squares.
  4. Each square is 1 hundredth, so 0.3 × 0.4 = 0.12
Why it works: “0.3 × 0.4” means “0.3 of 0.4”. A tenth of a tenth is a hundredth, so tenths × tenths give hundredths. The product (0.12) is smaller than both factors, because you are taking part of a part.

Decimal × Decimal: Placing the Point

Goal: I can count decimal places to place the point in a product.
Show how

Solve 2.5 × 1.4.

  1. Multiply as whole numbers: 25 × 14 = 350. (25 × 10 = 250, 25 × 4 = 100, 250 + 100 = 350.)
  2. Count decimal places in both factors: 2.5 has 1, 1.4 has 1. Total: 2.
  3. Put 2 decimal places in the answer: 350 → 3.50, which is 3.5. Too few digits? Write zeros in front: 0.2 × 0.3 → 6 → 0.06.
  4. Check by estimating: 2.5 × 1.4 is a bit more than 2.5 × 1 = 2.5, and less than 3 × 2 = 6. 3.5 fits.
Why it works: tenths × tenths = hundredths (6.4). 2.5 × 1.4 = 25 tenths × 14 tenths = 350 hundredths = 3.50. Count places before dropping trailing zeros.

6.6

Estimate Products

Goal: I can round to estimate a product and catch a misplaced decimal point.
Show how

Estimate 4.8 × 3.1. Is 14.88 reasonable?

  1. Round each factor to the nearest whole number: 4.8 ≈ 5, 3.1 ≈ 3.
  2. Multiply the rounded numbers: 5 × 3 = 15.
  3. Compare: 14.88 is close to 15, so it is reasonable. An answer of 1.488 or 148.8 would mean the decimal point is in the wrong place.
Why it works: the rounded factors are close to the real ones, so the product is close too. Estimating is the fastest way to check where the decimal point goes.

Multiplication Word Problems

Goal: I can tell when a story needs multiplying, then solve and label.
Show how

One notebook costs $2.35. How much do 4 notebooks cost?

  1. What is asked? The cost of 4 equal groups of $2.35: multiply. (“Each”, “per”, “equal groups”, “times as much”, and “of” for part of an amount: 0.5 of 3.2.)
  2. Estimate: 2 × 4 = about $8.
  3. Multiply: 235 × 4 = 940. Two decimal places: 9.40.
  4. Check and label: $9.40 is close to $8. Four notebooks cost $9.40.
Money tip: money keeps 2 decimal places, so write $9.40, not $9.4.
